Title: Quasi-bound states in the continuum based tuneable metasurfaces

In this talk I will discuss two kinds of switchable metasurfaces based on quasi-Bound States in the Continuum (q-BIC) resonances. The first one resonates in the middle infrared, a spectral region relevant for single molecule detection relying on infrared absorption. The metasurface switching happens due to the temperature-induced phase transition of a tiny vanadium dioxide layer composing the metasurface's metaatoms. The second kind of metasurface resonates in the near infrared spectral region, and consists of a simple homogeneous amorphous silicon thin film. The q-BIC resonances are generated by spatially modulating the film's refractive index by letting two ultrafast laser pulses superpose in space and time at the film. We show ultrafast switching response times of the q-BIC resonance, which is limited by the diffusion time of charge carriers in the conduction band of the semiconductor film.

Prof. Josy Ossarima UFPI

Title: From Photoluminescence to Nonlinear Phenomena: New Perspectives on Rare-Earth-Doped Materials

Materials doped with trivalent rare-earth ions constitute an important class of photonic systems because of their unique spectroscopic properties and wide range of applications in lasers and random lasers, light-emitting devices, optical sensing, and photoluminescence thermometry [1]. This talk will present recent results involving energy upconversion processes, laser and random-laser emission, photon avalanche, and nonlinear phenomena observed in rare-earth-doped nanoparticles [2]. The discussion will address how the interplay between optical and thermal processes can induce threshold behavior, abrupt transitions, and significant changes in the spectral properties of these materials [3]. Finally, applications in optical thermometry and perspectives on the use of artificial intelligence techniques to optimize photoluminescent sensors will be presented [4].

Title: INOVAÇÃO EM EMBALAGENS PARA ALIMENTOS: DESAFIOS E OPORTUNIDADES EM ENGENHARIA DE MATERIAIS

Optical Machine Learning exploits the physical properties of light, such as propagation, diffraction, interference, and modulation, to perform computations involved in machine learning algorithms. The central idea is to replace, either fully or partially, electronic operations, particularly matrix-vector multiplications, with optical processes that can potentially offer very high speed, massive parallelism, and lower energy consumption. In this presentation, we will first introduce the fundamental concepts behind machine learning algorithms and discuss how they can be implemented using optical systems. We will then present experimental optical setups that demonstrate the implementation of such algorithms. Finally, we will focus on an optical system based on optical speckle patterns as a physical realization of the random projection in a machine learning framework, investigating how the system's performance depends on the statistical properties of the speckle patterns.

Title: What can universities do about water pollution? Photocatalytic materials as part of the answer

Water pollution caused by pharmaceuticals, dyes, and other emerging contaminants remains a major environmental challenge because these compounds can persist in aquatic systems and affect ecosystems and human health. Addressing this problem requires new treatment technologies supported by scientific research, infrastructure, collaboration, and the training of qualified professionals. Universities contribute by developing materials and processes capable of removing or degrading these contaminants and by connecting fundamental science with environmental applications. In this lecture, we will discuss the environmental context of water remediation and present examples of research conducted at LabSiNaP in collaboration with partner universities. The presentation will focus on photocatalytic materials and strategies involving ion doping, control of synthesis conditions, natural gums, and clay-mineral supports, showing how university research can contribute to the development of more effective technologies for water remediation.

Igor Alessandro Silva Carvalho HORIBA Scientific

Title: Raman e NanoRaman/Nanofotoluminescência: espectroscopia multimodal colocalizada para caracterização físico-química de amostras

This Esta apresentação abordará as técnicas de Raman e NanoRaman aplicadas aos mais diversos ramos do conhecimento. Nela, você conhecerá os princípios básicos da espectroscopia AFM-Raman aplicada à caracterização química de uma ampla gama de materiais, incluindo polímeros, cerâmicas, biomateriais, amostras das ciências da vida e materiais bidimensionais (2D). A microscopia Raman e NanoRaman é uma das poucas técnicas capazes de fornecer análises precisas e não destrutivas, combinadas com imagens de alta resolução. A espectroscopia Raman fornece informações valiosas sobre a amostra estudada, como sua composição química e estrutural. Com base na interação luz-matéria, obtemos informações relevantes — como distribuição de partículas, homogeneidade, tamanho de grão, mudanças de fase e outras características — por meio da avaliação química do material. A integração (ou técnica híbrida) da Microscopia de Força Atômica (AFM) com a espectroscopia Raman proporciona uma compreensão aprofundada da dinâmica complexa de diversos tipos de amostras; ao reconhecer a importância de uma caracterização abrangente, essa abordagem viabiliza a análise nanoestrutural e química do espécime, incluindo a detecção de contaminantes e materiais em nível de traços. A HORIBA apresentará uma solução de caracterização multimodal baseada em uma plataforma AFM automatizada e integrada a um espectrômetro Raman/Fotoluminescência, permitindo medições verdadeiramente colocalizadas de propriedades físicas e químicas. Nesse contexto de colocalização, a espectroscopia Raman facilita a identificação e a caracterização de diversos compostos, fornecendo dados essenciais sobre suas estruturas moleculares e composições, inclusive quanto a potenciais contaminantes em microescala. Simultaneamente, o AFM contribui com informações vitais sobre as propriedades topográficas e mecânicas dessas amostras, oferecendo dados detalhados sobre texturas superficiais, adesão, rugosidade e rigidez em nanoescala. Discutiremos também a combinação de imagens de fotoluminescência excitada por laser e espalhamento Raman em cristais bidimensionais (2D) para revelar a estrutura no estado sólido. O avanço da instrumentação possibilita a integração do Raman com outras técnicas, como Fotoluminescência e AFM, permitindo alcançar resoluções em escala nanométrica. A técnica pode ser aplicada em diversas áreas, como indústria farmacêutica, fotovoltaica, grafeno, células, nanopartículas e microplásticos, entre outras.

Title: Quantitative Lensless On-Chip Microscopy Based on Quadriwave Lateral Shearing Interferometry

Quantitative phase microscopy (QPM) enables label-free investigation of transparent biological samples through optical path measurements. However, conventional QPM systems are bulky and complex, limiting point-of-care applications. This work presents a lensless on-chip QPM platform based on Quadriwave Lateral Shearing Interferometry (QLSI). The system integrates a 2D (0-pi) phase grating directly onto a digital sensor, illuminated by a RGB LED. Single-shot wavefront reconstruction is achieved through Fourier-based demodulation of the resulting interferogram. The platform operates in two configurations. In the Contact Regime (CR), the sample is placed directly on the grating. Validations demonstrate this mode accurately reconstructs wavefronts for low-contrast structures >100 μm with phase gradients below the Incidence Angle Threshold (IAT = 5.41 mrad). In the Geometric Magnification Regime (GMR), the sample is placed at a distance to project a magnified shadow. This configuration increases both the IAT and system bandwidth, enabling the characterization of objects as small as 10 μm, such as polystyrene microspheres. The quantitative agreement between experimental and numerical results confirms this platform's potential for high-resolution point-of-care diagnostics.

Title: Clay Minerals for Drug Delivery and Environmental Remediation

Clay minerals have attracted growing interest as multifunctional materials for pharmaceutical and environmental applications owing to their unique physicochemical properties. Their large specific surface area, ion-exchange capacity, chemical stability, biocompatibility, widespread availability, and low cost make them ideal candidates for the development of sustainable and scalable technologies consistent with circular economy and sustainability principles. In pharmaceutical sciences, clay-based materials are increasingly employed as drug delivery platforms capable of enhancing the stability, solubility, and bioavailability of poorly water-soluble drugs. Their ability to host and protect active compounds enables the design of controlled-release systems with improved therapeutic performance. In addition, several clay minerals exhibit intrinsic antimicrobial activity or can serve as carriers for bioactive molecules, essential oils, antibiotics, and anti-inflammatory agents, opening new opportunities for the development of safer and more efficient pharmaceutical formulations. Envronmental remediation represents another major field of application for clay minerals. Their remarkable adsorption capacity allows the efficient removal of a wide range of contaminants, including heavy metals, organic pollutants, and pharmaceutical residues from water and soil. Furthermore, the surface modification of clays provides tailored materials with enhanced selectivity toward specific pollutants and supports the development of advanced photocatalytic and decontamination technologies. Recognizing the scientific and technological potential of these materials, the International Network on the Use of Clays for Drug Delivery and Environmental Remediation (ASFARMED) was established in 2022 through an initiative of the University of Seville. The network currently brings together more than 100 researchers from six countries, encompassing 19 accredited research groups, 12 universities, 5 technological research centres, and 5 industrial partners. Organized through 24 coordination nodes and 15 active working groups, ASFARMED promotes multidisciplinary collaboration in research, development, and innovation through joint projects, researcher mobility, and advanced training activities. Its research agenda contributes directly to seven of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). ASFARMED's research activities span a broad range of topics, including controlled drug delivery systems, stabilization of bioactive compounds and essential oils, enhancement of drug photostability, innovative pharmaceutical formulations, removal of emerging contaminants, development of hydrogels and superabsorbent materials for sustainable agriculture, environmental photocatalysis, recovery of high-value compounds from agro-industrial by-products, and computational modelling of clay–bioactive molecule interactions. Through these efforts, ASFARMED has become an internationally recognized platform fostering excellence and innovation in the applied research of clay-based materials.

Title: Light-matter quantum correlations and the development of quantum technologies in Recife-Brazil

The formation of collective entangled states on atomic ensembles is heralded by detection of photons spontaneously emitted by the sample. Its origin is the coherent interaction of atoms with the quantum reservoir of vacuum modes. In the last two decades, many groups have explored this process to devise applications in quantum information. Here I present our recent work on the problem, exploring quantum correlations generated from simple systems, like cold ensembles of two- and three-level atoms. I also review other works of our group with implications for quantum technologies, like in photon pair generation, ultrafast control, and multipartite entanglement. Finally, I present the recent projects of our group to develop quantum technologies in Brazil.
